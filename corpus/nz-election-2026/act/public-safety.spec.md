# What the page states

> The Accredited Security Operator licence, private-premises powers, three-year exclusion notices, reasonable force limits, consent-based searches, twice-as-many security officers as police comparison, and Retail NZ $2.6 billion crime cost are all on the source page. No licence fee or rollout timetable is given.

System: Accredited Security Operators for public and business safety

Scenario: A licenced Accredited Security Operator responds to disorder on private premises
Given New Zealand has twice as many licenced security officers as police officers but they currently have no more authority than any other citizen
And Police cannot respond quickly to every call
And retail crime costs the sector $2.6 billion a year according to Retail NZ's Crime Report
When a new licence is introduced that security professionals can obtain with training
Then licenced Accredited Security Operators may require a person's name and address on private premises and detain them until Police arrive if they refuse and will not leave
Output conduct-based exclusion notices enforceable for up to three years across multiple premises held on a register under the Privacy Act
And removal of people who threaten safety, cause damage or breach a venue's conditions of entry
And reasonable and proportionate force as a last resort, ceasing when the person complies or Police arrive
And consent-based safety searches on premises where refusing a search means no entry
Outcome retailers, business associations, shopping strips and transport operators may use operators with extra powers
And private premises have trained operators with extra powers until Police arrive
