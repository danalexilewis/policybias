# Our understanding

> Queues are treated as a staffing problem. Grants and a workplace lift are meant to keep people in the job so beds can open. Dental care is pulled toward the same needs rule as the rest of care. Regions must still find the staff, and adults still face an unpriced dental safety net.

System: Staff hours as a queue instrument

Scenario: A workplace lift is meant to open care beds
When working hours are shortened, the waiting-day deduction is removed and specialist training is paid for
Then more people are expected to stay and more beds to open
Outcome queues fall if the extra workforce actually arrives
But schedule cover and training time must be paid from grants the policy does not dimension

Scenario: Public money is fenced off from profit and fast-track insurance
When surpluses must be reinvested and private insurance cannot buy a jump in public care
Then publicly funded slots should follow need
Outcome a private fast track loses its public foothold
But privately insured patients lose that exit and wait in the same queue
