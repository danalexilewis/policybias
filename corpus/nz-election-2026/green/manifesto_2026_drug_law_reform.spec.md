# What the page states

> Alcohol and other drug policy would shift from punishment toward health and harm reduction. The Misuse of Drugs Act would be replaced with regulated cannabis access, expanded harm reduction and addiction support, psychedelic treatment licensing, and Good Samaritan laws with naloxone and supervised consumption spaces. Nicotine availability would phase down, drug driving law would move to impairment testing, and alcohol advertising and sponsorship would phase out with levy-funded harm reduction. No dollar amounts or dates are given.

System: Health-based alcohol and other drug policy

Scenario: The drug law reform plan is put into effect
When the government puts the drug law reform plan into effect
Then the Misuse of Drugs Act is repealed and replaced with an evidence-based approach treating drug use as a health issue and providing regulated access to cannabis for personal use
And funding for harm reduction and addiction support including kaupapa Māori, rainbow and youth programmes is ramped up
And local government and service providers are supported in town centres impacted by drug use
And a licensing system for psychedelic treatment is enabled and native psilocybin species receive taonga status
And Good Samaritan laws pass and naloxone availability and medically supervised consumption spaces are funded
And vaping and smoking cessation services are funded, nicotine availability is phased down and retailer regulation is strengthened
And the Land Transport (Drug Driving) Amendment Act 2025 is replaced with an impairment approach
And alcohol advertising and sponsorship are phased out with alcohol levy funding a national harm-reduction campaign
Outcome people can seek help without stigma or fear of punishment
