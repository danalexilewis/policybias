# What the page states

> Game Animal Council landing-site designation, DOC access tests, Outdoor Access Commission priority, CAA aircraft safety handover, and commercial take restrictions by gender are all on the source page. No figures or implementation dates appear.

System: Backcountry access for hunting and venison recovery

Scenario: Access decisions are made for public conservation land in rugged country
Given much of New Zealand's public land is difficult to reach and in rugged country such as Fiordland a helicopter can be the only practical way in
And large areas remain closed by no-fly and no-landing lines with no clear conservation or safety reason
And DOC can dictate the type of helicopter commercial venison recovery operators use despite Civil Aviation Authority aircraft safety regulation
When backcountry access rules are reformed
Then the Game Animal Council may designate sensible and accessible helicopter landing sites
Output DOC required to use clear, public and consistent tests when deciding where aircraft may land
And the Outdoor Access Commission directed to make hunting access a priority in its work programme
And DOC stopped from dictating which helicopters commercial venison recovery operators use, with aircraft safety left to the Civil Aviation Authority
Outcome the Game Animal Council may restrict commercial take by animal gender when necessary so recreational hunters and recovery operators fairly share a well-managed resource
And access restrictions require a clear conservation or safety reason rather than old map lines alone
