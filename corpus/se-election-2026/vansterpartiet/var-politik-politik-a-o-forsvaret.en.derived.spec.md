# Our understanding

> Membership is kept, and so are the constraints that make membership thinner on the ground: no nuclear weapons, no permanent foreign troops. Conscription is asked to supply people. Three sectors — military, civil, welfare — are each told they need more, with only "a whole-society view" to rank them.

System: Alliance membership with a thinner host-nation offer

Scenario: A nuclear ban meets alliance planning
Given membership in Nato cannot replace the home force
When a new law forbids nuclear weapons on Swedish territory
Then transit, visits and storage need a legal answer
Outcome the Nordic nuclear-free zone is the political line
But planners in the alliance still work with nuclear tools the host has banned on its soil

Scenario: Conscription, civil defence and welfare bid for the same kronor
Given the military and civil branches both need more, and so do many other parts of society
When the number of conscripts rises
Then trainers, barracks and kit must expand
Outcome popular anchoring grows if the intake is broad
But a clinic, a shelter or a regiment waits if the whole-society ranking is not written
