# Our understanding

> Two krona amounts land on families. A GDP share lands on public investment. The carbon price and wealth-tax rates — the instruments meant to pay for both — are not printed. Indexed municipal grants then follow costs automatically.

System: Named transfers, unpriced carbon

Scenario: Family transfers rise with named krona amounts
When child benefit and a single-parent supplement rise by the printed amounts
Then those households get a standing monthly uplift
Outcome pressure from food and housing should ease for families with children
But housing benefit is promised only a rise, not a krona amount, so that part stays unpriced

Scenario: An investment rule as a GDP share is added to the fiscal framework
When public climate and welfare investment is aimed as a share of GDP each year
Then the state commits to a flow of capital spending that grows with the economy
Outcome the surplus target is no longer the only fiscal anchor
But if the surplus target still binds something else in the budget must give way, and that something is not named
