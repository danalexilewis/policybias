# Our understanding

> Recognising valued introduced species in law ends pest-by-default treatment, so control now needs a local damage case first. Opening suitable Crown land is access plus a conservation management duty, and the biodiversity credit pilot needs a unit, a buyer and a double-counting rule before it can pay.

System: Valued species status, Crown-land access, and an unnamed credit

Scenario: Valued introduced species are recognised in law
Given valued introduced species can be treated as pests by default today
When a coordinated wild animal management framework formally recognises them in law
Then they are no longer pests simply because they were introduced
Outcome management turns on local conditions and unacceptable damage
But control that used to run on a pest label now needs that damage case first

Scenario: Suitable Crown land opens for hunting and fishing
Given recreational hunting generally prevented on Crown land managed by Land Information New Zealand today
When suitable Crown land ordinarily open to the public is opened under rules similar to public conservation land
Then hunters gain access they did not have
Outcome hunter-led conservation and game management become part of how that land is used
But access without monitoring, herd plans and conflict rules is a management gap, not only a win

Scenario: A biodiversity credit is piloted for hunter-led conservation
When a government-backed biodiversity credit is piloted
Then payment needs a measurable unit, a buyer, and a rule against selling the same gain twice
Outcome hunter-led conservation is named as the environmental benefit
But until those rules exist, the pilot cannot pay
