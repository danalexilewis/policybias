# Our understanding

> The policy states a special economic zone covering the former refinery and Northport, with planning and RMA consents decided by a government panel, and tax incentives only under consideration. It does not commit a tax rate, budget, or date. The zone is planning relief first.

System: Special economic zone as planning relief before fiscal incentives

Scenario: The zone is established at Marsden Point
Given industries in the zone would otherwise deal with local and regional councils, DOC and other agencies under the RMA
When a special economic zone including the former refinery and Northport is established
Then planning rules and RMA consents are determined quickly by a government panel
Outcome energy generation and maritime activity including ship repair are prioritised in the zone
But the principal committed relief is planning speed, not a named tax cut

Scenario: A consent that would have gone to local decision-makers arrives
Given local and regional councils and other agencies would otherwise hold RMA and planning roles
When the government panel decides the consent on economic growth and zone aims
Then the local veto over that activity is removed
Outcome consents turn on the panel's growth and zone tests
But ratepayers and councils no longer hold the final planning say inside the zone

Scenario: Tax and investment incentives are weighed after the zone exists
When tax incentives, Overseas Investment Act changes and rates rebates are considered
Then those options remain under consideration rather than committed
Outcome the zone can still operate on planning relief alone
But any fiscal concession is not yet a promise a business can bank on
