# Our understanding

> The policy creates infringement offences for deliberately standing in a lawful hunter's line of fire and for using drones or helicopters to harass game-bird shooters. It does not state fine amounts, evidence standards, or how aircraft harassment is detected.

System: Infringement for safety-risk interference, not for opposition

Scenario: Someone deliberately stands in a lawful hunter's line of fire
Given hunters acting safely and within the law can be deliberately obstructed by people who step into the line of fire with no specific infringement offence
When deliberately standing in the line of fire of a lawful hunter becomes an infringement offence
Then that conduct carries a specific legal consequence
Outcome hunters expected to follow strict safety rules are not undermined by deliberate obstruction
But without a stated fine or evidence rule, how hard the offence bites stays unknown

Scenario: Drones or helicopters are used to harass a game-bird shoot
Given drones or helicopters can be used to harass game-bird shooters by scattering or flushing birds with little legal remedy
When an infringement offence is created for using drones or helicopters to harass game-bird shooters
Then scattering or flushing birds to sabotage a lawful shoot is priced as an offence
Outcome disturbance of birds and air-ground hazard get a named legal answer
But enforcement still needs a way to attribute the aircraft and prove intent to harass

Scenario: Someone campaigns against hunting without entering the line of fire or flying a harassing aircraft
When specific infringement offences are created for line-of-fire obstruction and aircraft harassment
Then opposition that stays off those behaviours remains free
Outcome the offences target interference that puts others at risk, not disagreement with hunting
But the boundary between protest near a shoot and obstruction will be argued case by case until practice settles it
