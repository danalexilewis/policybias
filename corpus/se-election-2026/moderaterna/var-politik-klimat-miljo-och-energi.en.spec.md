# What the page states

> Sweden has the EU's lowest emissions per person and should reach net zero by 2045 at the latest. Dispatchable electricity should be rebuilt in the south so that households do not have to live by the electricity price. Climate funds should go where emissions fall most per krona. The Baltic Sea should be protected against bottom trawling and eutrophication.

System: Dispatchable power, cost-effective emissions and protected seas

Scenario: Southern Sweden lacks dispatchable electricity
Given six reactors have been closed early
And southern Sweden lost 3,800 MW of dispatchable electricity production
When new nuclear power supplies southern Sweden
Then hydropower can be used for heavy industry in the north
And the state should reduce risk through regulation, financing and state ownership
And electricity price, grid fees and energy taxes should be kept collectively as low as possible
And capacity charges should be reformed so that ordinary families do not have to plan life around the power system
And dispatchable power sources should be paid for their availability
And investment in the Kraftlyftet support scheme should be increased
Outcome electricity should be available where it is needed when it is needed

Scenario: Sweden should reach net zero and protect the Baltic Sea
Given Sweden accounts for around 0.1 percent of global emissions
And more than half of the world's dead seabeds are in the Baltic Sea
When Klimatklivet and Industriklivet reward innovation and electrification
Then the climate premium for light lorries should be extended and Klimatklivet strengthened for charging infrastructure in homes and apartment buildings
And inefficient environmental permitting should be simplified
And large-scale industrial trawling for fishmeal should be stopped and the Baltic Sea should get a pause
Output 1.5 million tonnes lower emissions by 2030 according to the budget
Outcome emissions should fall where each krona achieves most and coastal fishing should be secured
