# Our understanding

> The policy amends the Treaty of Waitangi Act, puts Te Tiriti-based decision-making across government, reforms settlements without Full and Final clauses, resources the Waitangi Tribunal with binding land-return powers, and explores private-land first refusal. It does not quantify Tribunal resourcing or define the binding power's appeal path. We treat binding private-land return as needing statute that reaches title, and Full and Final removal as keeping claims open.

System: Binding return and open-ended settlements

Scenario: The Tribunal gains binding land-return recommendations
When Waitangi Tribunal resourcing increases with power to make binding recommendations for return of Crown and private land
Then Crown land can move on a binding path if the statute holds
Outcome land return is no longer only recommendatory in the policy's terms
But private land only moves if the law can compel private title; otherwise the recommendation stops at the owner's gate

Scenario: Full and Final clauses are removed from settlements
When Treaty Settlements reform removes clauses that refer to Full and Final settlement
And large natural grouping ends and hapū rangatiratanga is validated
Then a signed settlement no longer closes further claim on Full and Final terms
Outcome more parties can negotiate at hapū scale
But claims that were treated as closed under Full and Final no longer have that closure on the reformed guide

Scenario: Private-land first refusal is only explored
When return of wrongfully alienated whenua includes exploring a right-of-first-refusal at point of sale
Then officials may design a process for iwi, hapū and whānau to buy first
Outcome the policy keeps the door open to private land return outside settlements
But exploration is not an operating first-refusal scheme until regulations or statute land
