# Our understanding

> Lawful private expression exits the disciplinary frame, neutrality is required, mandatory training is limited, and misconduct powers stay. Professional-body power narrows: the private-expression lever is removed while the workplace lever remains.

System: Regulator power narrowed to work, not private opinion

Scenario: A professional publishes a lawful opinion in their own time
Given more than 200,000 New Zealanders work in regulated professions
And teachers, nurses, real estate agents and other professionals have faced disciplinary action or compulsory ideological training over opinions expressed outside their work
When the law bans regulators from disciplining members for lawful expression outside professional practice
Then that private expression is no longer a professional offence
Outcome registration no longer turns on agreeing with the regulator's politics
But speech still tied to professional practice can remain disciplinable

Scenario: A regulator sets compulsory training
When mandatory training is restricted to competence and public safety
Then ideological training outside that frame cannot be compelled as a registration condition
Outcome the regulator's syllabus shrinks to the job
But what counts as competence or safety still sits with the regulator until a court says otherwise

Scenario: Serious harm to a client or patient still occurs at work
When regulators retain power against professional misconduct, harm to clients or patients, and breaches of professional duties
Then workplace misconduct can still end a career
Outcome public protection at work remains
But the boundary between private opinion and professional duty becomes the live dispute
