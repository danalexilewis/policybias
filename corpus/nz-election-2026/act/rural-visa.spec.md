# What the page states

> Seasonal visa pathways do not fill year-round farm labour shortages holding back export industries. The Rural Workforce Visa ties workers to accredited rural employers for three-year terms with readvertise-and-reissue renewal and a Work to Residence pathway after six cumulative years, exempt from the infrastructure levy. No accreditation criteria or commencement date is given.

System: Rural Workforce Visa for primary sector employers

Scenario: An accredited rural employer fills a year-round farm role with a migrant worker
Given New Zealand's farms, orchards and fishing fleets generate $60 billion in exports every year
And rural communities face chronic labour shortages the current immigration system is not equipped to solve
And seasonal visa pathways do not address year-round roles that keep farms running every day
When the Rural Workforce Visa is created as a standalone three-year visa for dairy, sheep and beef, and general farm work
Then the visa is attached to accredited rural employers rather than a geographic region
Output workers may transfer to any other accredited rural employer without a new application but cannot move to non-rural sectors
And an initial three-year term with no renewal required within that period
And before the visa term ends employers readvertise and if no suitable New Zealander is available the visa is reissued for a further three years
Outcome a new agricultural stream on the Work to Residence Visa makes workers who have held an RWV for 72 cumulative months with an accredited employer and meet standard requirements eligible for residence
And the Rural Workforce Visa is exempt from the infrastructure levy where applying it in rural communities would make no sense
