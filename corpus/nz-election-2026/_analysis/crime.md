# Crime, justice and public safety

Reduces fourteen crime-tagged party dump pages into proposed events versus status-quo framing.

## national

### Given

Judges must take good-character testimony into account at sentencing for sexual offending (`news-20260517-honpaulgoldsmith.md`). The Crimes Act lists what consent is not but does not define affirmative consent (`news-20260719-honpaulgoldsmith.md`). National cites prior term changes: a 40% cap on sentencing discounts, victim control of permanent name suppression, and stalking as a jailable offence.

### When

Prohibit judges treating good character as a mitigating factor at sentencing for all sexual offending. Amend sexual-consent law so consent must be actively given — a communicated “yes” by words or actions — not inferred from silence, a previous relationship, or a prior encounter; burden of proof and reasonable-belief defence unchanged.

## act

### Given

Repeat burglars often receive non-custodial sentences; security guards lack powers beyond ordinary citizens; rural crime reporting and prosecution rates are low; family-violence offenders can use pets as coercion; child-safety checks are employer-run snapshots; teachers lack a quick removal power below stand-down; lawful hunters can be obstructed without a specific offence; resident-visa deportation liability is shorter than ACT proposes (`burglary.md`, `public-safety.md`, `rural-crime.md`, `stopping-coercion.md`, `keeping-children-safe.md`, `disrupt-the-class-leave-the-class.md`, `protect-lawful-hunting.md`).

### When

Create a burglary three-strikes regime: minimum three-year prison term, no parole, home detention, or early release on a third burglary conviction (or one conviction with three or more counts); aggravated burglary counts under both burglary and violent three-strikes. Licence Accredited Security Operators to require name and address on private premises, detain non-compliers until Police arrive, issue up-to-three-year conduct-based exclusion notices on a Privacy Act register, remove unsafe entrants, and use proportionate force and consent-based entry searches. Fund Police for rural patrol hours (target 100,000 extra hours per year), a dedicated rural crime unit, Text 111 for patchy mobile coverage, and stronger poaching/rustling offences. Amend the Crimes Act to criminalise using companion-animal abuse for family-violence coercion (up to seven years), tighten protection orders over pets, require pet recording on family-harm reports, and give Police statutory power to remove at-risk animals. Replace fragmented child-safety checks with a national Working with Children Card, continuous Police monitoring, immediate suspension on serious charges, and mandatory child-protection training. Amend the Education and Training Act 2020 so teachers may direct seriously disruptive students to a staffed space, escalate refusals, use reasonable force to remove after escalation, mandate stand-down or suspension after assaults (Police Youth Aid referral if criminal), fine non-engaging parents up to $300 then $3,000, and recover deliberate property damage from parents. Create infringement offences for deliberately standing in a hunter’s line of fire and for using aircraft or drones to harass game-bird shooters. **Crime slice only (full plan → immigration cluster):** deport resident visa holders convicted of offences carrying sentences of 10 years or more regardless of time in New Zealand — beyond the Government’s 20-year proposal (`deport.md`).

### Money

Burglary victims 184,000 in 2025 — context stat, not programme budget (`burglary.md`). Retail NZ estimates retail crime costs $2.6 billion per year — cited sector harm, not ACT spend (`public-safety.md`). Rural target 100,000 extra patrol hours per year; Police hiring cost unstated (`rural-crime.md`). Coercion offence penalty up to seven years imprisonment (`stopping-coercion.md`). Working with Children Card fee about $108 for paid workers; volunteers/students exempt or discounted (`keeping-children-safe.md`). Parent fines up to $300 (first offence) and $3,000 (later) (`disrupt-the-class-leave-the-class.md`). Hunting infringements and deport-rule fiscal cost unstated.

## green

### Given

Greens frame current policy as prison packing, restricted judicial discretion, and under-funded rehabilitation and violence prevention (`manifesto_2026_justice_and_community_safety.md`, `manifesto_2026_violence_prevention.md`, `manifesto_2026_drug_law_reform.md`). Government has stalled Te Aorerekura and cut community-led violence services.

### When

**Justice:** abolish mandatory minimums, life sentences, preventive detention, three-strikes, limits on sentence discounts, and restrictions on home detention; raise the age of criminal responsibility; end military-style academies; ban detaining young people with adults; extend hate-speech law to religion, gender, disability, and rainbow communities; make protection and parenting order applications free; increase the Steps to Freedom Grant for at least 30 years of inflation (no dollar amount). **Violence:** fund Te Aorerekura implementation; amend the Crimes Act to criminalise coercive control, redefine consent, and preclude a “reluctant consent” defence. **Drugs:** repeal and replace the Misuse of Drugs Act with a health-based regime including regulated personal cannabis access; replace the Land Transport (Drug Driving) Amendment Act 2025 with an impairment approach; pass Good Samaritan laws and expand naloxone and supervised consumption spaces.

### Money

Unstated on all three manifesto chapters (`money: no-figure`).

## Findings

1. ACT carries the densest rule-change programme: mandatory burglary sentencing, private security powers, rural policing targets, pet-coercion offences, national child-safety card, classroom removal powers, and hunting infringements — mostly without programme budgets.
2. National’s two 2026 justice releases name concrete sentencing and consent-law changes but no new spending figures; they extend a punitive sentencing track (good-character ban) alongside affirmative-consent definition.
3. Green manifesto chapters name wide statute repeals and amendments (three-strikes abolition, coercive-control offence, Misuse of Drugs Act replacement, drug-driving impairment test) but no costings — qualitative service expansions sit in slogan backlog.
4. Consent-law fork: National defines affirmative consent while preserving prosecution burden; Green would also amend consent and criminalise coercive control — overlap on rule change, divergent on sentencing and drug regimes.
5. Sentencing fork: ACT adds a burglary three-strikes minimum; Green would abolish three-strikes and other mandatory minima — direct collision.
6. Te Pāti Māori `justice.md` is directional only — no named statute, figure, or programme start/stop.
7. ACT `deport.md` criminal-deportation rule (10+ year sentences, no time limit) is the only crime-tagged immigration pointer here; full six-point plan deferred.
