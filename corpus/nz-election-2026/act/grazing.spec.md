# What the page states

> Wilding conifers and unmanaged vegetation fuel loads are spreading on conservation land while farmers face barriers to grazing concessions. The policy amends the Conservation Act to let DOC issue grazing licences with ecological frameworks, practical conditions and virtual fencing recognition. No dollar savings or implementation date is given.

System: Targeted grazing on conservation land

Scenario: DOC issues grazing licences on conservation land for vegetation management
Given wilding conifers choke 1.8 million hectares of land
And DOC manages large areas of open country, former pastoral land and other non-forest conservation land that could be safely grazed under controlled conditions
And farmers seeking grazing concessions are often met with resistance or unnecessary barriers
And taxpayers pay for expensive aerial spraying and manual cutting under current rules
When the Conservation Act 1987 is amended to give DOC explicit authority and direction to issue grazing licences for specified areas where grazing supports vegetation management objectives
Then a clear ecological framework identifies where grazing is appropriate to suppress conifer seedlings and other invasive vegetation while reducing wildfire fuel loads
Output grazing licences specifying appropriate stocking types, rates and duration
And virtual fencing systems recognised as a compliant method of stock exclusion on DOC land
Outcome invasive plants and pests are suppressed through targeted grazing instead of helicopter spraying alone
And fuel loads that contribute to devastating wildfires are reduced
