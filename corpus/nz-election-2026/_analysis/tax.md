# Tax and fiscal instruments

Reduces eleven tax- and fiscal-tagged party dump pages into proposed events versus status-quo framing. Instruments kept separate: Labour CGT levy versus small-business GST/write-off/payment rules; Green wealth/CAT/corporate/bank/tech/income package; National fiscal rules; ACT bureaucracy consolidation; TPM GST-on-kai.

## labour

### Given

New Zealand is one of few countries without a capital gains tax; Australia introduced one in 1985 (`capitalgainstax.md`). Labour frames too much wealth locked in property rather than productive investment, and one in six New Zealanders unable to afford a doctor. Nine in ten New Zealanders will not pay the proposed CGT (do not own more than one property or a commercial property).

Investment Boost mainly benefits larger companies for investments many would have made anyway; an IRD survey found 53% of respondents said it had no effect on 2025 investment, 24% reported an increase, and 7% a significant increase (`small-business-action-plan.md`). The GST registration threshold has been $60,000 since 1 April 2009 (previously $40,000). Small businesses make up 97% of New Zealand businesses. Government agencies are already committed to paying 95% of domestic invoices within 10 working days.

### When

**CGT (levy).** Introduce a 28% capital gains tax on profit from commercial or residential investment property, realised only on sale after 1 July 2027. No tax on gains accrued before that date (value at 1 July 2027 sets the taxable base for later sales). Exempt the family home (including lifestyle blocks), farms, inheritances, gifts, KiwiSaver, shares, business assets, and personal items (cars, boats, art, furniture). Exempt small businesses selling premises to buy a larger one. Improvements remain deductible where premises are owned. Ring-fence every dollar raised to health, including Medicard free GP visits (`capitalgainstax.md`; stated Medicard spend only via `medicard.md` pointer — do not fold visit rules into this note).

**Small-business fiscal instruments (separate from CGT).** Within the first 100 days, introduce legislation requiring large businesses to pay small suppliers within 15 days for invoices of $25,000 or less, and restore Business Payment Practices Act 2023 payment-time disclosure. A large business is one with revenue above $33 million (ex-GST) and operating expenditure above $10 million in each of the prior two financial years, excluding wages, salaries, and intra-group payments; the rule also applies to government. Let small businesses report late payments to MBIE with proportionate penalties (repeated late payment → larger fine).

From 1 July 2027, lift the low-value asset write-off threshold from $1,000 to $10,000 for businesses with annual revenue below $10 million (~500,000 eligible); full deduction in the year of purchase (timing benefit versus multi-year depreciation). From 1 July 2028, lift the compulsory GST registration threshold from $60,000 to $80,000 (~35,000 businesses between those bands); voluntary registration remains below the threshold. Fund the package by refocusing Investment Boost toward small businesses.

### Money

CGT rate 28%, aligned with company tax (`capitalgainstax.md`). CGT gross revenue — **unstated** on the CGT page; do not invent revenue to match Medicard spend.

Medicard programme spend funded by CGT (pointer only): $393.3 million in 2027/28, rising to around $553 million a year once fully in place (`medicard.md`).

Small-business package $1.56 billion over four years, of which the asset write-off is $1.355 billion (`small-business-action-plan.md`). GST threshold change and payment-time rules — **unstated**.

## national

### Given

National cites seven years of deficits, negative sovereign-credit outlook from Fitch and Moody’s before Budget 2026, and rising global borrowing costs (`responsibility-rules.md`). As of BEFU 26, net core Crown debt is forecast to peak at 46.1% of GDP in 2027/28 and fall to 44.4% by the end of the forecast period. When Labour took power in 2017, core Crown expenditure was around 28% of GDP; by late 2023 it had risen to 34%. National states it achieved $50 billion in savings across the last three Budgets (historical record of this term, not a 2026 programme offer). Budget 2026 brought the forecast return to surplus forward one year.

### When

Maintain three fiscal rules: (1) return to operating surplus by 2028/29 and keep surpluses sufficient to bend the debt curve down; (2) bend net core Crown debt down below 40% of GDP and keep it below 40% over time; (3) keep taxes low by right-sizing government, with core Crown expenditure declining toward 30% of GDP (`responsibility-rules.md`).

Reach surplus through spending restraint, not new taxes on workers. Use revenue upside (as at Budget 2026) to reduce the deficit; do not trigger sharp frontline cuts on downside surprises. Hold tight operating allowances; fund additional frontline spend via reprioritisation and cuts to lower-priority expenditure. Continue capital investment offset where possible by capital savings and value-for-money tests; consistent surpluses create room to borrow for infrastructure while still bending debt down.

### Money

$50 billion past savings — historical record in Given, not a forward programme figure. Debt path: peak 46.1% of GDP (2027/28) → 44.4% by end of forecast; target below 40%. Expenditure-share target 30% of GDP core Crown. No new tax revenue assumed — **unstated** scale of future reprioritisation needed to hit the rules.

## green

### Given

Greens frame gross inequality: the wealthiest 150 rich-listers own more than half the country; multinationals (Facebook, Google, Amazon cited) pay little while small local businesses face IRD pressure (`greens_commit_to_tax_the_super_rich_and_large_corporates_fairly_cut_income_tax_for_96.md`). Release compares multimillionaires paying $9 per $100 earned versus a teacher paying $22 — **sketch**, not a programme budget. Luxon Government prioritised landlord and corporate tax cuts over liveable incomes (`manifesto_2026_income.md`).

### When

**Wealth and acquisitions.** Introduce a 2.5% annual tax on net assets above $10 million, exempting family homes. Implement a Capital Acquisitions Tax on assets and gifts received above $1 million, exempting family farms and family homes (`tax_system_for_all.md`, release).

**Corporate, bank, tech, landlords.** Return corporate tax to 33% for the 0.7% largest corporations (supermarkets, energy, banks); keep 28% for small and medium enterprises. Introduce a 0.06% bank levy on liabilities of the four big banks (Australia-style). Enforce a 5% withholding tax on offshore profits sent by big tech. Reverse Luxon’s landlord tax cuts and bright-line test changes (`tax_system_for_all.md`, release).

**Personal income.** Create a $10,000 tax-free income threshold (96% of people receive a cut). Add a new higher income-tax rate on earnings above $160,000 — rate unstated on dump pages (`tax_system_for_all.md`, release). Manifesto income chapter (tax instruments only): make income tax progressive via a tax-free threshold, lower rates for low- and middle-income earners, and a higher top rate; introduce a 2.5% super-rich tax on net wealth above $10 million (`manifesto_2026_income.md`). Named thresholds and the higher top rate stay in When even where the rate figure is missing.

### Money

Net revenue increase $5.15 billion in 2027/28, rising to $5.73 billion by 2030/31 (`greens_commit_to_tax_the_super_rich_and_large_corporates_fairly_cut_income_tax_for_96.md`; corrected reissue 22 June 2026). Per-instrument revenue splits — **unstated**. Higher rate above $160,000 — **unstated**. Lower middle-income rates in manifesto — **unstated**. Teacher-versus-multimillionaire comparison — **sketch**.

## act

### Given

ACT cites 78 ministerial portfolios, 28 ministers, and 43 departments versus Norway’s 20 ministers and 17 ministries for a similar population. Sprawling bureaucracy is framed as expensive and as blurring accountability; overseas research is cited that more ministers with spending power correlate with bigger deficits (`affordable-government.md`).

### When

Consolidate 43 departments into 19 coherent departments. Collapse 78 portfolios and 28 ministers into 18 ministers, with each department reporting to one minister for its budget and outcomes. Let ministers appoint departmental chief executives for a fixed term (renewable once) and remove them for specified reasons such as non-performance or policy misalignment, with public-service protections and a right to return to a lower classified role. Remove the Public Service Commission. Protect operational independence by law for Police, NZSIS, and NZDF. Frontline nurses, teachers, and police are stated as unaffected (`affordable-government.md`).

### Money

**Unstated.** Page claims bureaucracy costs crowd out taxpayers, debt repayment, and frontline spend — **sketch**, no savings figure and no numbered spend-cap dollar amount.
