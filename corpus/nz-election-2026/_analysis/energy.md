# Energy and household power

Reduces eight energy-tagged party dump pages into proposed events versus status-quo framing. Named intervention per party below.

## labour

**Intervention:** SolarSaver (Government-backed household solar finance and subsidies).

### Given

Power bills up 20% in two years under the current Government (**sourced**, `solarsaver.md`). Typical rooftop solar and battery system costs around $20,000 upfront (**sourced** as party figure). The Gas Security of Supply fund is almost entirely unspent (**sourced** claim). Plug-in solar is constrained by regulations that have not kept pace with the technology (**sourced** claim). Existing affordable finance requires a mortgage and enough equity (**sourced** claim).

### When

**Lines-company loans.** Borrow through the local lines company for solar and battery; repay gradually through the power bill; own the system from day one; early repayment allowed at any time. Interest rate unstated beyond “low-interest”. Loan size/cap unstated.

**Ratepayer Assistance Scheme (RAS).** Separate property-linked low-interest loan covering solar, batteries, and other home electrification (e.g. hot water heat pumps). Designed by councils, Local Government New Zealand, and Rewiring Aotearoa. Loan stays with the property on sale; early repayment allowed. Interest rate and loan cap unstated.

**Kickstart subsidy.** Up to $3,000 for low- and middle-income households, including renters — enough for a full plug-in kit or to reduce rooftop install cost. Exact income thresholds unstated.

**Plug-in solar regulation.** Change regulations so renters can use plug-in panels that connect to a normal power point.

**Fair export rates.** Set fair time-of-use rates when households export power to the grid. Named cents/kWh unstated.

**Community battery fund.** Councils, iwi, community trusts, charities, and lines companies may apply; Government covers half of each project (up to $200,000). Lines companies may build, own, and operate batteries in energy-vulnerable neighbourhoods or to defer network upgrades.

**Delivery and skills.** EECA runs a free one-stop shop (system selection, installer quotes, finance matching, whole-of-life costs). Tertiary Education Commission coordinates workforce growth with industry (apprenticeships, re-skilling, micro-credentials).

**Start.** EECA shop front, subsidy scheme, lines-company loans, and RAS loans up and running within 12 months of taking office.

### Money

**Sourced** package: $160 million over four years, funded by repurposing the Gas Security of Supply fund (`solarsaver.md`): $77 million household subsidies; $30 million community batteries; $22 million EECA delivery; $20 million Crown underwrite for lines-company loans; $7 million to establish RAS; $4 million workforce coordination. Kickstart ceiling $3,000. Illustrative two-panel renter kit ~$1,500 saving $300–$400 per year (pays for itself in four to five years) — **sourced** as Labour examples, not audited outcomes. Lines-company and RAS interest rates, maximum loan sizes, and expected take-up — **unstated**.

- `corpus/nz-election-2026/labour/solarsaver.md` — https://www.labour.org.nz/election-policy-pages/solarsaver

## national

**Intervention:** Home Energy Fund (narrower Ratepayer Assistance Scheme) plus small-scale renewable permitted-activity rules.

### Given

This term National cites RMA reform, ending Labour’s oil and gas ban, securing coal/diesel/LNG reserves, and four renewable projects consented through Fast Track (**sourced** framing, `solar.md`). Small-scale renewables still face fragmented, costly consent rules across regions (party examples: Environment Canterbury water-discharge consent for ground-mounted solar runoff; Hurunui consent for free-standing installs larger than 25 m²). Commercial bank green loans are limited relative to upfront cost (**sourced** claim).

### When

**Home Energy Fund (narrower RAS).** Central-government and council-backed financing platform jointly owned by the Crown and participating councils. Fund pays the approved installer upfront; homeowner repays a low-interest loan over ten years via a rates charge or on sale of the property. Loans secured against the property; available to ratepayers with at least 20% equity. Council participation voluntary (LGNZ advocacy cited for expected uptake). Eligible uses: residential solar and battery storage; insulation, heat pumps, and other residential electrification. Requires amendment to the Local Government Act. Interest rate unstated beyond “low, competitive”. Total lending capacity unstated.

**Permitted-activity electrification rules.** National permitted-activity settings so property owners may install, without a consent (subject to standard effects safeguards where named): rooftop solar even where height limits or landscape overlays would otherwise apply; small-scale on-farm ground-mounted solar; small-scale battery storage not enclosed by an existing building; small run-of-river micro-hydro for on-site use below a set size with fish-passage and water-user conditions. Exact size thresholds for micro-hydro and ground-mounted solar beyond the Hurunui example — **unstated**.

Do not merge with Labour’s RAS: National names no kickstart subsidy, no lines-company loan channel, no community battery fund, and no EECA delivery appropriation.

### Money

**Sourced:** one-off Crown equity investment of $7 million for a 20% shareholding in the Home Energy Fund entity; balance from participating councils and the LGFA (`solar.md`). Loan fund size, interest rate, and fiscal risk if defaults cluster — **unstated**. Consent-rule changes — **unstated** (regulatory, not a named spend).

## green

**Intervention:** Kiwipower plus household clean-energy loans and named community / public-housing / Māori-housing spend (launch triplet); manifesto market-structure and fossil rules remain qualitative.

### Given

Four big power companies control over 85% of retail and generation (**sourced**, `power_for_all_of_us.md` / launch page). More than 200,000 households cannot afford to heat their homes (**sourced** claim). Warmer Kiwi Homes already exists; Greens claim they established it nearly 20 years ago (**sourced** claim, launch page). Tangata whenua are two to three times more likely to face energy hardship (**sourced** claim, launch page). Luxon Government LNG terminal framed as locking in imported fossil fuels (**sourced** claim).

### When

**Kiwipower.** Establish a publicly owned Crown entity to invest in new renewable generation and contract firming capacity, including dry-year supply, and to support new independent market players. Capitalisation and build timeline unstated.

**Zero-interest clean energy loans.** Government-backed scheme for solar and battery installs covering up to 90% of homeowners. Loan cap, eligibility beyond homeownership share, repayment term, and delivery agency — **unstated**.

**Export pricing and renter solar.** Ensure fair export prices for rooftop solar (cents/kWh unstated). Urgently legalise plug-in solar and establish a renters’ right to solar.

**Warmer Kiwi Homes expansion.** Expand the existing programme to cover more upgrades, including gas transitions, for more low-income households. Incremental appropriation unstated.

**Community and housing renewables.** Invest in community-owned renewable energy (schools, marae, papakāinga, libraries cited on launch page). Put solar on more than half of all public homes within four years; mandate solar on new or renovated public homes where appropriate. Invest in renewable energy for Māori housing.

**Manifesto energy chapter (named rules, no dollars).** Put public-wellbeing outcomes first in energy legislation; establish a renewable energy infrastructure agency; enable greater distributed solar and batteries for households, marae, and suitable public buildings with community-led energy planning; ban new fossil-fuel extraction, end fossil-fuel subsidies, and cancel the LNG terminal; establish a single energy regulator and a Ministry for Energy with Te Tiriti-based frameworks for Māori and community ownership; work with industry on decarbonisation, demand response, and efficiency. These stay in When; they are not a household-loan programme.

### Money

**Sourced** named spend (`power_for_all_of_us.md`, launch page): $200 million for community-owned renewable energy; $80 million for renewable energy for Māori housing; solar on more than half of all public homes within four years (stock share target, not a dollar figure). Zero-interest loans for up to 90% of homeowners — programme envelope **unstated**. Kiwipower capitalisation **unstated**. Warmer Kiwi Homes expansion **unstated**. Manifesto fossil ban, regulator, Ministry, infrastructure agency — **unstated**. Launch-page claim that corporations and the super-rich will fund the package — **sketch** (no amounts; tax detail belongs elsewhere).

## nz-first

**Intervention:** Gentailer structural split and market-rule rewrite; National Subsurface Development Survey; Marsden Point SEZ energy/planning slice.

### Given

The big four power companies control almost 90% of generation and sell back to themselves (**sourced**, `breaking_up_the_gentailers.md`). The most expensive generator sets the price for all electricity (**sourced** claim). New Zealand is isolated and dependent on international oil and gas supply lines (**sourced** claim, `future_proofing_our_fuel_security.md`). Existing seismic data described as two technology generations old, covering only a fraction of basins (**sourced** claim). Industrial closures cited as electricity-cost casualties (party list on gentailer page).

### When

**National Energy Strategy.** Deliver a National Energy Strategy for New Zealand. Scope and statute unstated.

**Gentailer split.** Split the big power companies into separate generators and retailers so they cannot control both supply and retail price.

**Market-rule rewrite.** Replace current rules so companies cannot withhold supply to inflate prices. Guarantee long-term fixed-price contracts for new-build generation and dispatch priority so that new generation is first used. Let home generators export at the same price they pay (symmetric export tariff). Implementation agency, timeline, and transitional arrangements — **unstated**.

**National Subsurface Development Survey.** Spend across the term to build one national dataset across energy, geothermal, and CO₂ storage sites and a framework to develop them. First results within twelve months; highest-ranked basins surveyed with wells identified within twenty-four months; pathway to domestic supply potentially within six years. Set explorer risk-bearing terms and a royalty well above 50% on mature extractions. Use finds to address gas shortfalls affecting power prices, lower local fuel costs, and (separately from household programmes) export and a Norwegian-model sovereign fund — last items are party framing, not household instruments.

**Marsden Point SEZ (energy/planning slice only).** Establish a special economic zone at the former refinery and Northport focused on energy generation/distribution and maritime activity. Principal benefit: relief from planning regulations and the RMA via a government panel deciding consents against zone growth aims, instead of local/regional councils, DOC, and other agencies. Tax incentives and OIA options may be considered — **one line, unstated**; not expanded here.

### Money

**Sourced:** $1 billion over the term for the National Subsurface Development Survey (`future_proofing_our_fuel_security.md`). Gentailer break-up, market-rule rewrite, symmetric export tariff, National Energy Strategy, and Marsden Point SEZ planning relief — **unstated**. Page claims of Norway-scale offshore potential and discovery odds well above 90% — **sketch**, not programme budgets.
