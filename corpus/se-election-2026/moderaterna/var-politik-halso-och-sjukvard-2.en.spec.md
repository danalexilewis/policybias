# What the page states

> Care is to be accessible, safe and coordinated so that the right care comes in time. The care guarantee is to be tightened and regions made liable to pay when it is not met. A national care brokerage is to make it easier to receive care in another region. Mental ill-health, involuntary childlessness and women's diseases are to be given priority.

System: Tighter care guarantee and national care brokerage

Scenario: A patient waits longer than the care guarantee
Given too many patients do not receive their treatment within the care guarantee's 90-day limit
And care queues fell by 28 percent from October 2022 to January 2026
And almost 25 billion kronor has been spent on shortening care queues during the parliamentary term
And a household with a police officer and a nurse pays around 130 000 kronor in regional tax each year
When the care guarantee is tightened and regions become liable to pay when it is not met
Then it is to become easier to receive care in another region through a national care brokerage
Outcome more people are to receive care in time without the regional tax being raised

Scenario: A child waits for BUP
Given waiting times in child and adolescent psychiatry are far too long
And in 2023 six in ten children and young people said they were troubled by anxiety, worry or angst
And 5.4 billion kronor is being put into psychiatry during 2026
When a statutory care guarantee is introduced for waiting time to a visit and treatment at BUP
Then first-line psychiatry is to be strengthened and relieve the pressure on BUP
Output 21 years as the new age limit for BUP instead of 18
And a named care contact for young people with severe mental illness
Outcome all children and young people are to receive help, support and care in time

Scenario: A couple seeks subsidised fertility treatment
Given involuntary childlessness affects around ten to 15 percent of the population
And around 100 000 children are born each year
When the number of subsidised IVF attempts is doubled and sibling attempts are included
Then more couples are to receive publicly financed fertility treatment
Outcome more people are to be able to become parents with subsidised treatment
