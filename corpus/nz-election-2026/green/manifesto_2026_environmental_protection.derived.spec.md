# Our understanding

> The policy sets ecosystem health limits with tangata whenua as shared decision-makers, repeals the Fast-track Approvals Act and revokes fast-track mining consents, restores a standalone Ministry for the Environment with more EPA and enforcement resource, updates air standards, and targets biosecurity eradications. It states no limit values, enforcement budget, or repeal timetable.

System: Limits and enforcement without priced capacity

Scenario: Fast-track mining consents are repealed and revoked
Given fast-tracked destructive mining in wild places under the Fast-track Approvals Act
When the Fast-track Approvals Act is repealed and fast-track consents for new coal, gold and seabed mining are revoked
Then those projects lose the fast-track consent path
Outcome te taiao is spared mining approved under that Act
But compensation and sunk cost for consent holders are unstated

Scenario: Ecosystem health limits bind decisions
When strong evidence-based limits for ecosystem health are implemented with Te Tiriti and tangata whenua as shared decision-makers and kaitiaki
Then development faces a health bottom line set with tangata whenua
Outcome rivers, forests and soils can be protected as the foundation of communities
But without stated limit values, the bottom line cannot be read as numbers from the policy

Scenario: EPA and enforcement resources increase
When a standalone Ministry for the Environment is restored and EPA and compliance, monitoring and enforcement resources increase
Then polluters face a better-resourced regulator
Outcome accountability can rise if investigations and prosecutions actually run
But without a budget figure, the enforcement lift is unmeasured
