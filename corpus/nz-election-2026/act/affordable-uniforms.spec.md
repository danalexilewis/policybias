# What the page states

> The $1,000-per-child spend, 38,000 WINZ hardship loans and $11 million figure, the three branded-item cap, generic alternatives, retailer choice, and exclusions for sports and kapa haka uniforms are all on the source page. No implementation date or compliance detail is given.

System: Affordable school uniforms through competition and choice

Scenario: State schools limit branded uniform requirements
Given many families spend well over $1,000 per child on shirts, socks, shorts or skirts, PE gear and replacements
And 38,000 WINZ hardship loans worth $11 million were issued in 2025 to help families meet uniform costs
And some state schools require everyday items from one approved supplier when equivalent items cost much less at ordinary retailers
When the Education and Training Act is amended to limit compulsory branded uniform items
Then state schools may require a maximum of three compulsory branded uniform items
Output three compulsory branded uniform items maximum at each state school
And generic alternatives for high-use items such as shorts, pants, skirts, skorts and socks
And families may buy generic items from standard clothing retailers
Outcome schools keep room for a small number of branded items that preserve identity and traditions
And sports uniforms, team strips, kapa haka uniforms and tracksuits remain outside the new rules
