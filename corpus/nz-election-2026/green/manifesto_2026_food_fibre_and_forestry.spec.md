# What the page states

> Food, fibre and forestry would shift from intensive short-term profit toward regenerative production that protects waterways and communities. Farmers would get transition finance, wool and other natural fibres would be used in public buildings, palm kernel expeller would be banned, and clear-fell controls and a National Soil Strategy would protect erosion-prone land. Local wood processing and Māori-led forestry on whenua Māori would expand, with a biodiversity credits scheme offering a voluntary ETS alternative. No dollar amounts or dates are given.

System: Regenerative food, fibre and forestry

Scenario: The food, fibre and forestry plan is put into effect
When the government puts the food, fibre and forestry plan into effect
Then low-interest loans and grants support farmers transitioning to more sustainable production
And Aotearoa-grown natural fibres including wool are used in schools and public buildings with a contestable fund for sustainable fibre manufacturing
And palm kernel expeller is banned and supplementary feed must be sustainably sourced
And clear-fell controls, erosion-prone land transition to indigenous forestry and a National Soil Strategy are implemented
And local wood processing manufactures higher-value timber products and biofuels from slash
And Māori-led forestry organisations are supported on whenua Māori with customary use rights recognised
And a biodiversity credits scheme offers a voluntary revenue alternative to the Emissions Trading Scheme prioritising marginal whenua Māori where agreed
Outcome the sector shifts from intensive short-term profit to long-term wellbeing
