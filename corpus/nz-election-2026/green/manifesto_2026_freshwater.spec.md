# What the page states

> Freshwater health would be restored through strong national bottom lines including a lower nitrate limit for drinking water and reinstated Te Mana o Te Wai. Subsidies for big new irrigation and dam projects would end, wastewater discharge rules would tighten to keep rivers and beaches swimmable, and the drinking water regulator would be funded for monitoring and enforcement. Flood resilience funding would rise, synthetic nitrogen fertiliser would phase out, and stocking rates would fall on a catchment basis. No dollar amounts or dates are given.

System: Freshwater health and drinking water safety

Scenario: The freshwater plan is put into effect
When the government puts the freshwater plan into effect
Then strong national bottom lines for freshwater are implemented including lowering the nitrate limit for drinking water
And Te Mana o Te Wai is reinstated to prioritise freshwater health and give effect to Te Tiriti o Waitangi
And subsidies and public funding for big new irrigation and dam projects end and consents are reviewed where water levels have dropped too far
And wastewater discharge rules are strengthened to keep lakes, rivers, beaches and coasts swimmable
And the drinking water regulator is funded for monitoring, reporting and enforcement
And flood resilience funding increases with room for rivers and wetland natural systems
And synthetic nitrogen fertiliser is phased out and stocking rates are reduced on a catchment basis
Outcome freshwater decline is reversed
