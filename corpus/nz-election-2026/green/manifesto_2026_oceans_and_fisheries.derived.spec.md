# Our understanding

> The policy protects at least 30% of ocean territory, bans destructive fishing methods and seabed mining, and reviews the Quota Management System. Quota value is not protected when waters close.

System: Closed waters and lost quota access

Scenario: Ocean territory is protected and fishing stops inside it
When marine protected areas legislation is reformed toward the ocean protection target
Then fishing stops or is tightly limited in the protected waters
Output at least 30% of New Zealand's ocean territory protected
Outcome ocean health and tikanga-led tools are the named aims
But quota holders who fished those waters lose authorised access there

Scenario: Destructive methods are banned
When bottom trawling on seamounts is banned along with set netting and dredging
Then vessels that relied on those methods cannot keep fishing the same way
Outcome seamounts and inshore habitats face less destructive gear
But operators and quota tied to those methods lose access or must retool at their own cost

Scenario: The Quota Management System moves to ecosystem management
When the Quota Management System is reviewed toward ecosystem-based fisheries management
Then authorised catch can be cut, moved, or reconditioned even when settlements stay in the frame
Outcome management aims at ecosystem health rather than single-stock extraction alone
But settlement and other quota holders absorb lost catch income with no compensation line
