# What the page states

> Classroom disruption consumes teaching time and contributes to teacher retention problems. The policy gives teachers explicit power to direct seriously disruptive students to staffed supervision, with escalation, reasonable force after refusal, mandatory response to assault, parent fines for missed meetings, and debt recovery for deliberate property damage. No implementation date or resourcing detail is given.

System: Classroom disruption response below formal stand-down

Scenario: A seriously or persistently disruptive student refuses to leave class
Given around half of teachers deal with disruption in every lesson
And Forty-seven per cent of teachers lose 40 to 50 minutes or more each day responding to challenging behaviour
And New Zealand had the worst behavioural climate in maths classes in the OECD in 2022 PISA results
And half of teachers told ERO that behaviour had a large impact on whether they intended to remain in teaching
When a teacher directs a seriously or persistently disruptive student to leave and report to a staffed, supervised space
Then a refusal is escalated to a dean or senior leader
Output staff may use reasonable force to remove a student who refuses a second lawful direction after escalation
And corporal punishment remains illegal
And removed students stay under supervision in a pastoral office, dean's room or learning support room, not empty or lockable rooms
And a parent who misses a mandatory meeting without reasonable excuse may be fined up to $300 for a first offence and up to $3,000 for a later offence
Outcome classroom teachers have an explicit legal power to direct a student to leave for serious or persistent disruption
And any assault on a teacher, student or staff member triggers immediate removal followed by mandatory stand-down or suspension, with criminal-threshold assaults referred to Police Youth Aid on the first occurrence
And a parent or guardian meeting is required when behaviour becomes serious or repeated or when a student refuses a lawful direction to leave
And schools may recover deliberate property damage costs from a student's parents as a debt if unpaid
