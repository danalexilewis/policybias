# Our understanding

> The conscription targets are counted. The tax that is to pay for them is not. Without a rate, welfare can be crowded out by the same rearmament the tax was to protect. Regiments in the north and an amphibious battalion on Gotland are markers that need people before they deter. Part-ownership in the defence industry is a second bill against the same unstated source.

System: An unstated tax against a counted headcount

Scenario: The preparedness tax is to carry rearmament
Given a preparedness tax that is to secure the permanent expanded financing
When the tax is introduced without a known rate
Then the source exists as a principle
Outcome welfare is said to be protected
But the protection cannot be counted

Scenario: Conscription grows towards the higher target
Given a target of 12 000 conscripts and in the longer run 20 000
When more people are called up
Then instructors and barracks are taken from the standing force
Outcome the headcount rises
But operational effect can fall while the training is under way

Scenario: The state enters as part-owner in the defence industry
Given state part-ownership in parts of the defence industry, for example SAAB
When the ownership is bought
Then capital is tied up in shares
Outcome control over security interests can increase
But the submarine and the air defence wait on the same till
