# What the page states

> Abusive partners often harm companion animals and many victims delay leaving to protect pets. The policy criminalises using companion animal abuse as family violence coercion, strengthens Protection Orders, requires Police to record pets on family harm reports, and gives explicit authority to remove animals to safety. No implementation timetable is given.

System: Protecting companion animals from family violence coercion

Scenario: Police respond to family violence where a pet is used as coercion
Given abusive partners were almost 11 times more likely to intentionally harm an animal than partners in non-abusive relationships
And more than half of victims delayed leaving an abusive relationship because they felt responsible for the safety of their pets
And a victim may escape a callout while the family pet is left behind with the alleged abuser
When the Crimes Act is amended to make using abuse of a companion animal as coercion or control in a family violence relationship an offence
Then the penalty may be up to seven years in imprisonment
Output Protection Orders strengthened so offenders cannot withhold, sell, give away or otherwise dispose of a protected person's companion animal
And companion animals not left with a dangerous abuser when a Protection Order is made
And Police required to record pets on family harm reports
And Police given explicit statutory authority and clear operational guidance to remove a companion animal to a place of safety such as Pet Refuge or the SPCA when the animal is at risk or used as coercion
Outcome victims can leave violence without leaving part of their family behind
And offenders cannot use pets as weapons of coercion
