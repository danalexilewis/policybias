# What the page states

> Crime prevention starts with welfare that catches young people early; society still sets punishment that matches the seriousness of the crime, without general tightening. Recruitment of children is to be stopped with school, social services, Sluta skjut across the country and a stop for drugs and weapons at the border. The prison service is in crisis with overcrowded remand prisons, so in-slucing, exit programmes and forensic psychiatry with special discharge review are to carry the relapse — not warehousing.

System: Welfare against recruitment and punishment according to seriousness

Scenario: New recruitment is met with welfare and the border, not with general tightening
Given gun violence and explosions are concentrated in socially vulnerable areas in the large cities
And a relatively small group of young men commit the largest share of the most serious crimes
And the Parliamentary Ombudsman has long criticised overcrowding in prisons and remand prisons
And prison sentences have generally become longer
When the recruitment of children into crime is met with school, social services and Sluta skjut

And the police may use infiltrators online but not crime provocation
And drugs and weapons are stopped already at the country's borders
And social intervention groups are set up in particularly hard-hit areas
Outcome children play football instead of acting as drugs couriers
And a national commission of inquiry is examined as a demand against gang violence

Scenario: Convicted people are channelled back while forensic psychiatry keeps high-risk cases
Given cooperation between social services, the prison service and care institutions does not work
When the state takes greater responsibility for channelling seriously criminal people back in
Then exit programmes with personal protection are developed so that people can leave crime and dare to testify
And violent and sexual offenders with a high risk of relapse can be kept in care with special discharge review
