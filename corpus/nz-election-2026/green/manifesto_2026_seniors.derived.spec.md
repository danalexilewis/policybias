# Our reading

> This is our systems reading of the source page, not the published claim. The page launches a long-term ageing strategy with a state-owned not-for-profit retirement village scheme, more community facilities, papakāinga and waitlist priority for kaumātua, and equitable home-care pay including travel time. It states no village capital, income rate, or care wage. Those readings are ours. We invent no dollar figures.

System: Ageing support without priced instruments

Scenario: A state-owned not-for-profit retirement village scheme is established
When a state-owned, not-for-profit retirement village scheme is established under a long-term ageing strategy
Then seniors can enter villages that are not run for private profit
Outcome ageing with dignity can sit outside the for-profit village model
But without capital, entry rules or an occupancy model, the scheme is named rather than built

Scenario: Home-care workers are paid including travel time
When home and community care workers are paid equitably and fairly including travel time
Then unpaid travel between clients ends as a wage gap
Outcome care quality can rise when workers are trained, supported and paid for the full job
But the page states no rate, so the cost lands on an unnamed funder or provider margin

Scenario: Kaumātua are prioritised for papakāinga and social housing
When more papakāinga are built and kaumātua are prioritised on social housing waitlists
Then older Māori move up the queue for warm, single-level, intergenerational homes
Outcome housing suited to kaumātua needs expands where builds and priority actually run
But without build targets, the priority rule competes with every other waitlist claim
