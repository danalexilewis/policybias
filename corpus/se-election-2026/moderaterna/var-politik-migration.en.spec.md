# What the page states

> Immigration should be tightened and control regained over who resides in the country, so that integration can succeed. Asylum-related immigration should fall towards the minimum level of EU law. Highly qualified labour should be prioritised. Those who commit crime or lack permits should leave.

System: Tighter asylum, higher wage requirement and more returns

Scenario: Asylum and labour immigration are tightened
Given between 110,000 and 185,000 people reside in Sweden without a valid permit according to the Tax Agency's calculations
And around 350,000 people are registered with the Public Employment Service
And first-time permits granted in 2023 amounted to less than 0.5 percent of the workforce
When asylum legislation is adapted to the minimum level of EU law and relevant EU legislation is reviewed
Then requirements for Swedish, civics, self-support and longer residence should apply for citizenship
And those sentenced to a penalty stricter than a fine should as a rule be deported
And the maintenance requirement should be raised towards the median wage with exceptions for certain occupations
And a population census, reception centres and more internal immigration controls should be carried out
And a duty to report information should cover the Public Employment Service, the Social Insurance Agency, the Prison and Probation Service, the Enforcement Authority, the Pensions Agency and the Tax Agency
Output just over 33,000 kronor
And 90 percent of the median wage
And 350,000 kronor in return grants for adults
And 25,000 kronor for children
And up to 500,000 kronor for spouses or cohabiting partners
And up to 600,000 kronor per family
Outcome asylum-related residence permits should be at the lowest level since 1985 and more people without permits should leave the country
