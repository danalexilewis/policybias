# Our understanding

> Synthetic nitrogen fertiliser is phased out and stocking rates fall by catchment. Farms lose a cheap input and run fewer animals. Rivers and drinking water get the relief. The timetable and the nitrate number come later.

System: Freshwater bottom lines as a farm input ban

Scenario: Synthetic nitrogen fertiliser is phased out
When synthetic nitrogen fertiliser is phased out
Then farms lose a major input that currently lifts pasture growth
Outcome rivers and drinking water face less nitrogen pressure
But farms lose a cheap input before any transition money arrives

Scenario: Stocking rates fall on a catchment basis
When stocking rates are reduced on a catchment basis
Then affected farms run fewer animals than today
Outcome catchment water quality can improve
But affected farmers run fewer animals before they know which catchments move first

Scenario: Bottom lines and Te Mana o Te Wai bind upstream land use
When strong national bottom lines are implemented including a lower nitrate limit for drinking water
And Te Mana o Te Wai is reinstated to prioritise freshwater health
Then land use upstream of drinking water and rivers must meet the harder rule
Outcome freshwater decline can reverse if the rule is enforced
But farms upstream of drinking water intakes must comply before the nitrate number is published
