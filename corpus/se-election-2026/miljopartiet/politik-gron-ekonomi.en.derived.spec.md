# Our understanding

> Two kronor figures land on families. A GDP share lands on public investment. The carbon price and the wealth-tax rates — the instruments that are supposed to pay for both — are not printed. Indexed municipal grants then follow costs automatically.

System: Named transfers, unpriced carbon

Scenario: Family transfers are lifted by named kronor amounts
When child benefit and a single-parent supplement rise by the printed amounts
Then those households receive a standing monthly top-up
Outcome food and housing pressure is meant to ease for families with children
But housing benefit is only promised a rise, not a kronor figure, so that part stays unpriced

Scenario: A GDP-share investment rule is added to the fiscal framework
When public climate and welfare investment is targeted as a share of GDP each year
Then the state commits to a flow of capital spending that grows with the economy
Outcome the surplus rule is no longer the only fiscal anchor
But if the surplus rule still binds, something else in the budget has to give, and that something is not named
